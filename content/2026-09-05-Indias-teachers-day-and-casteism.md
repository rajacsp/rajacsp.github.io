September 5th is Teachers' Day, marking the birthday of Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan — philosopher, statesman, and India's second President. He once described casteism as a cancer afflicting India. In that spirit, this piece revisits his views on the subject and asks that we treat Teachers' Day as an occasion to confront India's foremost social ailment.

## Five Aspects of Radhakrishnan's Views on Casteism

**1. Distinguishing Varna from Jati**
Radhakrishnan drew a sharp line between the original varna system, which he read as a classification rooted in guna — innate qualities, aptitude, and temperament — and the later jati system, which hardened into rigid heredity. He held that the ancient varna concept was meant to be fluid and function-based rather than birth-based, and that its calcification into hereditary caste was a historical corruption of Hindu social ideals.

**2. Opposition to Untouchability**
He was unequivocally against untouchability and the discrimination it carried. He saw it as a moral stain on Hindu civilization and backed its abolition, believing, like many reformers of his era, that spiritual Hinduism had to be cleansed of such social hierarchies.

**3. Philosophical Hinduism over Ritual Hierarchy**
Radhakrishnan consistently foregrounded the philosophical and mystical dimensions of Hinduism — the Upanishads, Vedanta, and the Gita — over ritual, priestcraft, and social rank. For him, the true essence of Hinduism was spiritual universalism, not caste orthodoxy.

**4. Reformist, Not Revolutionary**
He was a moderate reformer rather than a radical social revolutionary in the mould of B.R. Ambedkar. Though he criticized caste and supported reform, he worked largely within a reinterpreted Hinduism rather than calling for its wholesale dismantling, favouring education and spiritual renewal over political agitation.

**5. In Practice**
As Vice-President and later President, he upheld the constitutional values of equality and non-discrimination. His personal life and public positions reflected a cosmopolitan, caste-transcending outlook.

## In Summary

Radhakrishnan opposed casteism, untouchability, and birth-based hierarchy, viewing them as deviations from Hinduism's true philosophical spirit. His approach was that of a scholar-reformer seeking to restore an idealized, merit-based social vision — not that of a revolutionary bent on destroying the system outright.

As Radhakrishnan dreamed of killing Casteism, let's end Casteism in India. Let the Modern India start with us!
