Mark Zuckerberg went from building a home messaging app at age 12 to running the world's largest social network. This is the story of how a self-taught programming prodigy turned a dorm-room project into a platform used by billions — and the controversies he weathered along the way.

![Young Mark Zuckerberg]({static}/image/2026-07-28-mark/young-mark.png)

## The Kid From Dobbs Ferry

**Born in New York** Mark Zuckerberg was born on 14 May 1984 just outside Manhattan, the only son of Edward (a dentist) and Karen (a psychiatrist, later stay-at-home mother), growing up with three sisters as the family's "prince."

**Early gifts** His love of mathematics and science showed young, earning him a place at the highly selective Phillips Exeter Academy, where he won prizes in mathematics, astronomy, physics, and classics.

**First code** His father taught him Atari BASIC. By 12 he had built ZuckNet, a messaging program his father used in his dental office and the family used at home. Zuckerberg would also turn his artist friends' drawings into games.

## Too Fast for His Tutors

**A private tutor** Recognizing his talent, his parents hired computer tutor David Newman to work with him once a week — though Newman eventually couldn't keep pace, as Zuckerberg was already taking graduate courses at Mercy College.

**Synapse** Still in high school, under the company name Intelligent Media Group, he built a music player called Synapse. Microsoft reportedly offered $9.5 million for it. He turned them down.

## The Harvard Years (Sort Of)

**Arriving with a reputation** He enrolled at Harvard in 2002 already known, per The New Yorker, as a "programming prodigy," studying psychology and computer science.

**CourseMatch** In his sophomore year he wrote CourseMatch, letting students pick classes based on others' selections and form study groups.

**FaceMash** Built over a weekend, it invited students to vote on who was "hotter" from paired photos. Harvard took it down by Monday amid complaints about unauthorized photo use, and Zuckerberg publicly apologized, calling it "completely improper."

## A Dorm Room That Changed the World

**The Harvard Connection pitch** In late 2003, Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss and Divya Narendra approached him about a social networking idea. He worked on it briefly before leaving to pursue his own.

**The launch** With Dustin Moskovitz, Chris Hughes, and Eduardo Saverin, he launched The Facebook on 4 February 2004 — Harvard-only at first, then expanding to schools like NYU, Columbia, Stanford, and Cornell.

**Going west** The team moved to Palo Alto, planned to return to Harvard, then dropped out as opportunity grew. Facebook had roughly 1 million users within months.

## Saying No to Billions, Building One Instead

**First major investment** In 2005, Accel Partners invested $12.7 million. Zuckerberg opened the platform to more colleges and high schools, and membership reached about 5.5 million by late 2005.

**Turning down buyers** Offers came from Yahoo! and MTV Networks, but he refused, citing a vision of "an open information flow for people" over corporate ownership.

**Global scale** By 2021 Facebook had about 2.8 billion active users, becoming the world's most powerful social network.

## The Lawsuit That Wouldn't Go Away

**The lawsuit** In 2006, Narendra and the Winklevoss twins claimed he had stolen the Harvard Connection idea. Investigation of his messages revealed links between the projects, and an initial $65 million settlement was reached.

**Further dispute** In 2011 the three claimed they had been misled about the value of their Facebook stock.

## From Dorm Room to World's Rich List

**The takeaway** Zuckerberg dropped out of Harvard to chase his vision, and Facebook's staying power owes much to its constant evolution. His story is a reminder that belief and persistence can carry a dream far — he started at 19 in a dorm room and became the youngest person on the world's rich list.
